Team CRA003 · Lead Architect: Atharv Namdeo · Affiliation: Indian Institute of Technology · Live: https://atharv-namdeo.github.io/3d-mapping/

1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

Physical indoor spaces (megastores, corporate headquarters, academic campuses, hospitals) present severe navigation and spatial orientation bottlenecks. Traditional static 2D blueprints fail to convey spatial volume, vertical clearance, or dynamic obstacle layouts. Existing industrial digital twin pipelines either mandate prohibitively expensive LiDAR surveying rigs (>$10,000) or produce non-navigable radiance fields that fail on consumer devices.

Namma Space introduces an automated, end-to-end spatial computing engine that converts ordinary smartphone camera video and inertial sensor streams into photorealistic, millimeter-accurate 3D digital twins and autonomous robotics navigation costmaps without auxiliary surveying gear. Delivered through a zero-install WebGL/WebXR client, it features desktop first-person FPS roaming (WASD), touch-optimized mobile virtual joysticks, 2D floor radar, and obstacle-aware A* pathfinding.

2. Dual-Stream End-to-End System Architecture

Monocular 3D spatial reconstruction suffers from a fundamental tension between visual photorealism and geometric watertightness: radiance fields (3DGS) provide view-dependent lighting but lack physical collision meshes, while classical meshing produces watertight bounds but suffers from melted-wax artifacts on flat surfaces. 3. Field Capture Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

Reconstruction fidelity in deep monocular SLAM depends critically on baseline parallax and frame sharpness. We formulate a mathematically repeatable SOP executed on consumer smartphones:

  • Sensor Configuration: 1080p/4K at 60 FPS. Auto-Exposure and Auto-Focus are hard-locked (AE/AF Lock) to prevent luminance shifts and focal scale breathing across frames.
  • Perimeter-Loop Pattern: The surveyor executes a smooth heel-to-toe gait along the room perimeter, angling the optical axis 45° inward toward the room centroid to maintain continuous parallax across wall-floor junctions.
  • 360° Island Orbits: Center-room obstacles (islands, conference tables, structural columns) are circumscribed with an inward-facing orbit at 1.2 m standoff distance.
  • Loop Closure Mandate: The terminal trajectory re-traverses the initial coordinate origin for ≥3.0 seconds, providing pose-graph constraints that neutralize cumulative drift.
  • Automated Blur Rejection: Blurry frames are rejected via Laplacian variance: Var(∇²I) = (1/WH) ∑ (∇²I(x,y) - μ_∇²I)² > 100.0. Subsequent keyframe decimation operates on camera displacement (|Δt| > 0.15m or |Δθ| > 8°).

4. Mathematical Foundations & Coordinate Frames

4.1 Pinhole Unprojection into Metric Camera Frame

Given pixel coordinate (u, v) with estimated metric depth z (in meters) and camera intrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy):

X_c = ((u - cx) * z) / fx, Y_c = ((v - cy) * z) / fy, Z_c = z P_c = [X_c, Y_c, Z_c, 1]^T

4.2 Coordinate Frame Conversion: OpenGL to OpenCV

ARCore coordinates follow OpenGL convention (+X right, +Y up, -Z optical forward). The SLAM pipeline follows OpenCV (+X right, +Y down, +Z forward). We map poses via diagonal transformation matrix:

T_GL_TO_CV = diag(1.0, -1.0, -1.0, 1.0) T_world_cv = T_world_gl · T_GL_TO_CV P_world = T_world_cv · P_c

4.3 Hardware Gravity Vector Lock (Eliminating Drift & Tilt)

Monocular SLAM without gravity constraints designates Frame 1 as the world origin (0,0,0). A 25° wrist pitch bakes a permanent 25° diagonal tilt into the room, while turns drift into banana curves. PoseCam locks the world frame directly to Earth's hardware gravity vector g = [0, -9.81, 0]^T via the phone's 200 Hz STMicro accelerometer, guaranteeing the floor is clamped horizontal at Y = 0.00 m.

6. Spatial Discretization & 2D ROS Navigation Costmaps

Continuous 3D spatial reconstructions are converted into safe, non-colliding navigation paths through explicit geometric occupancy modeling.

6.1 Geometric Vertical Height Bandpass Slicing: [0.08m, 1.80m]

To prevent erroneous obstacle registration, points are filtered within a strict vertical elevation band:

  • Ground Noise Exclusion (h < 0.08 m): Door sills, low carpets, floor trim, and power cords are classified as walkable floor datum (y = 0.00 m).
  • Overhead Fixture Exclusion (h > 1.80 m): Hanging chandeliers, ceiling fans, structural beams, and HVAC ducting are eliminated.
  • True Collision Obstacle Band (0.08m ≤ h ≤ 1.80m): Student desks, chairs, walls, structural pillars, and cabinets are mapped as occupied cells.

6.2 2D ROS Costmap Generation & Navigation Server Specification

Filtered obstacle points are orthographically projected onto a 2D discrete grid with a metric resolution of s = 0.05 m/pixel (5 cm grid cells). Occupancy values are mapped to standard ROS 2 Nav2 format:

# floormap.yaml (ROS 2 Nav2 Map Server Spec) image: floormap.pgm resolution: 0.050000 origin: [-4.250000, -3.100000, 0.000000] negate: 0 occupied_thresh: 0.65 free_thresh: 0.196

6.3 Obstacle Dilation & Catmull-Rom A* Pathfinding

To account for robot / avatar collision volume, occupied costmap cells are dilated by an avatar clearance radius r_clear = 0.30 m. A* calculates the optimal shortest path across collision-free cells, which is interpolated into a continuous 3D navigation floor ribbon using Catmull-Rom cubic splines.

7. Zero-Install Web Client & Mobile WebXR AR

The platform client runs purely client-side across mobile and desktop browsers with zero installation:

  • Rendering Engine: Three.js rendering 2.81M points at a locked 60.0 FPS on standard integrated laptop GPUs (Intel Iris Xe / Apple Silicon) and mobile browsers.
  • Dual Controls: Desktop FPS PointerLock (WASD, Shift sprint at 3.4 m/s, Space jump, C crouch, procedural footstep audio) and mobile virtual touch joysticks (MOVE + LOOK).
  • Live Floor Radar HUD: Real-time synchronized 2D floor radar displaying player coordinates, speed, and heading orientation.
  • Client-Side Drag & Drop: Allows testing local .ply models in-browser without server upload roundtrips.
