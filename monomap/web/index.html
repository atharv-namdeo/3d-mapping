Champion Model 1 & Comparative Analysis of 10 Formats

We engineered and benchmarked 10 distinct 3D representations from the same sensor capture. Here is why Model 1 (Dense Metric Cloud) was chosen as the submission champion, and the specific failure modes of the other 9 candidates.

🏆 SUBMISSION CHAMPION · BEST ARCHITECTURAL CHOICE

Model 1: Dense Metric Point Cloud

Rather than forcing noisy polygonal triangles across untextured walls and empty spaces, Model 1 directly unprojects Depth-Anything-V2 metric depth maps into a fused, denoised 2.81-million-point coordinate cloud.

100% Photorealistic RGB: Captures whiteboard text, ceiling fans, power cables, and desk textures without polygon blurring. Zero Melted-Wax Warping: Eliminates erroneous triangular bridges across doors, open aisles, and chair legs. Locked 60.0 FPS WebGL Rendering: Uses single-draw-call buffer geometry with GPU point splats, running silky-smooth even on mobile browsers.

The Other 9 Evaluated Models & Technical Limitations

MODEL 02 REJECTED FOR VISUALS
Trimmed Poisson Mesh
Why Rejected: Screened Poisson reconstruction attempts to build closed water-tight isosurfaces. In indoor rooms, it creates "melted wax" triangles across unobserved door openings and rounds off sharp table corners.
MODEL 03 REJECTED FOR GAPS
Ball-Pivoting Mesh (BPA)
Why Rejected: While BPA prevents ballooning into empty space, uniform white walls with sparse visual features leave prominent triangular pinholes and perforated meshes where points are sparse.
MODEL 04 REJECTED FOR BRIDGING
Alpha-Shapes Concave Hull
Why Rejected: The alpha radius parameter $\alpha$ produces non-physical convex bridge sheets between distinct student chairs and desks, blocking walkable aisles in navigation tests.
MODEL 05 REJECTED FOR SOFT EDGES
TSDF Marching Cubes
Why Rejected: Volumetric zero-crossing Marching Cubes produces clean outer boundaries, but the 3cm voxel discretization blurs sharp monitor bezels, power sockets, and ceiling fan rods.
MODEL 06 REJECTED FOR SIMPLICITY
Planar CAD BIM Shell
Why Rejected: RANSAC planar fitting isolates floor, ceiling, and primary perimeter walls, but discards interior furniture, computers, and whiteboard annotations entirely.
MODEL 07 ROBOTICS COLLISION ONLY
3D Voxel Lattice
Why Rejected for Visuals: Generates blocky Minecraft-style $5\text{ cm}^3$ cube voxels. Excellent for 3D drone/robot collision checking, but visually inappropriate for human digital twin walkthroughs.
MODEL 08 AI SEGMENTATION ONLY
LingBot-Map GCT
Why Rejected for Visuals: Encodes geometric curvature tensors (Gaussian & Mean curvature) into vertex colors. Highly valuable for autonomous semantic segmentation, but replaces natural RGB photorealism.
MODEL 09 SEMANTIC ONLY
Digital Twin CAD Boxes
Why Rejected for Visuals: Represents recognized objects as colored wireframe 3D bounding boxes. Perfect for IoT asset inventory, but lacks visual surface realism.
MODEL 10 HIGH DRAW CALLS
Oriented Surfels Disks
Why Rejected: Renders tangent surface circular disks. While superior to triangles for preserving room openings, surfels require 3× higher draw call overhead in WebGL than continuous point clouds.

Autonomous Robotics

Spatial Discretization & 2D ROS Costmaps

Converting continuous 3D digital twins into obstacle-free 2D occupancy grids for autonomous robotics navigation via height bandpass slicing.

2D ROS Occupancy Grid (floormap.png)

Standard ROS 2 Nav2 costmap. White represents obstacle-free walkable floor, black represents occupied walls and desk boundaries, and gray represents unobserved exterior areas.

Resolution: 0.05 m/px Occupancy Threshold: 254 Format: PGM / PNG / YAML
# floormap.yaml (ROS 2 Nav2 Map Server Spec)
image: floormap.pgm
resolution: 0.050000
origin: [-4.250000, -3.100000, 0.000000]
negate: 0
occupied_thresh: 0.65
free_thresh: 0.196

Height Bandpass Slicing: [0.08m, 1.80m]

Points are filtered within a strict vertical elevation band: floor cables, door sills, and carpets below 8 cm are ignored, as are chandeliers and ceiling lights above 1.80 m. Only physical human obstacles (desks, chairs, walls) are marked occupied.
