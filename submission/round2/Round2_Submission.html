3. Evaluation criteria

Spatial indexing & POI tagging

POIs are points or floor polygons in a metric, gravity-aligned frame. A hash grid answers radius and nearest queries; zones are bucketed by bounding box and confirmed by point-in-polygon. A click is resolved by marching the camera ray through the obstacle heightfield derived from the scan — no mesh is needed — then picking the point there, else the smallest zone containing it.

Pathfinding accuracy

The reconstruction is turned into a 5 cm walkability grid: a cell is walkable only if floor was observed there and nothing occupies the 0.12–1.8 m band above it. Unseen space is never assumed free. Routes are planned on cells with enough clearance for a body, so they cannot clip a table corner or squeeze through a gap narrower than a person.

0.01 %of cells marked walkable are actually solid (synthetic office, exact ground truth)
< 0.5°gravity error re-aligning a capture tilted 17° and scaled 0.21×
every 2 cmeach test route is sampled and must keep body clearance
≈ 75 msto plan the 76 m hostel route from the lobby to the stairs (laptop CPU, one core)

Search UI & transition

Results update on every keystroke with walking distance and time; unreachable places are labelled as such. Selecting previews the route; “Jump” flies the camera to a viewpoint chosen to face the place with a clear line of sight. Deep links (?poi=…) open the app already routed.

Code cleanliness & repository setup

  • No build step: ES modules served as static files. python3 -m http.server and open /app/.
  • Logic (app/src/core) is separate from rendering (view) and DOM (ui), so it runs and is tested in Node.
  • 46 automated tests in GitHub Actions: 37 for grid, planner, search, spatial index, guidance and every shipped scene (each POI valid, searchable and reachable on foot); 9 for the pipeline against ground truth.
  • Reproducible: python -m navkit build app/scenes/<id> regenerates every derived file from the reconstruction.

4. Pipeline

PoseCam capture (our ARCore recorder: every frame’s pose in metres, gravity up) → navkit posecam, CPU only: per-frame depth (Depth Anything V2) placed with the ARCore poses; the depth scale is measured by triangulating image features against ARCore’s motion (hostel: 154 frame pairs, scale 0.572), cross-checked by the floor (phone 1.33 m above it); frames fuse into 4 cm voxels seen from ≥ 2 views → navkit build: floor plane, wall squaring, walkable grid + obstacle heightfield (the walked path counts as floor), automatic floor plan, web-ready points → web app. The hostel took ≈ 6 min of depth on 4 CPU cores and 10 s to build the navigation data. Gaussian-splat reconstructions go through the same navkit build for a photoreal view.

5. Scenes and honest limits

  • Hostel — our own capture: one 4-minute PoseCam walk (Nothing Phone A059), 174 m, lobby + three corridors, 179 m² of walkable floor, 16 tagged places and zones. Metric scale from ARCore. Drawn as oriented surface discs so walls and floors read as continuous surfaces; not photoreal (640×480 frames). The uploaded session had frames 0–5,499 of 6,908, so the last corridor leg is not mapped. Individual room numbers were not readable and are not yet tagged.
  • Home — a Gaussian-splat scene from our teammate’s training workspace, kept as a photoreal example. Scale estimated (about ±10 %); splat scenes can instead use a one-measurement calibration (floor-to-ceiling height), tested to within 1 % on ground truth.
  • Office — 24 m × 16 m, synthetic, built from a known layout to measure accuracy and show multi-room routing. Clearly labelled as synthetic in the app.
  • One flat floor per scene; stairs, ramps and tiered seating are not yet handled.
  • Round 1’s per-frame PoseCam clouds were too noisy to navigate (under 4 % of points on a floor plane). The fused, scale-measured pipeline above replaces them; the builder still rejects unusable input with an explanation instead of producing a bad map.
  • AR navigation (bonus) is planned for Round 3 and is not part of this submission.
